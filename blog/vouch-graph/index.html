Vouch Graph: hiring contributors by who they shipped with

16 Nov 2026 · built for the HydraDB hackathon (Track 1, the self-improving graph) · live page · code · 62-second demo

I've run community ops and contributor rewards for DAOs for four years. The hardest call every month isn't paying people. It's deciding who to ask. When a community needs someone to host a Yoruba onboarding call, the job usually goes to whoever is loudest in the Discord, or a friend of the lead.

The trust that should decide it does exist, but it lives in people's heads: "Ngozi ran the onboarding calls with me, and Femi translated the roadmap with her." Nobody writes that down, so nobody can search it.

Search makes it worse. Put everyone's bio in a vector store and ask "who can host a Yoruba onboarding call?", and you get the best-written bio. In my sample data that's Tunde: "Yoruba native speaker and world-class community host. Hosted 100+ onboarding calls." His only record in the community is a no-show.

What I built

Vouch Graph stores who shipped what, with whom, and how it went. It answers with a path, not a similarity score:

You → Ngozi ("Weekly newcomer onboarding calls")
    → Femi ("Yoruba translation of the Q4 roadmap")

When a hire happens, the outcome goes back into the graph, so the next answer is better.

The graph is small and deliberate:

Person ──CLAIMS──▶ Skill            what their bio says
Person ──SHIPPED──▶ Work ──NEEDED──▶ Skill   what they actually did (outcome: delivered / late / no-show)
Person ──WORKED_WITH──▶ Person      who shipped together, with event_time

A question goes through three steps:

  1. SelfGraph's retrieve() seeds entities from the question and walks the current relations stored in HydraDB.
  2. Code scores each person from the returned subgraph: the skill fit of the work they shipped, times its outcome, plus a bonus for a trust path. That path is someone who shipped with you (one hop), or with someone who did (two hops).
  3. Every hire outcome is inserted as a new versioned fact, with its own event_time.

Because nothing is deleted, retrieve(..., as_of="2026-10-31") replays what the graph would have answered before.

How HydraDB and SelfGraph helped

  • SelfGraph gave me versioned facts for free. "Bisi delivered on 15 Nov" doesn't overwrite history. It becomes the newest version, and as_of can still see the October picture. For a hiring tool, that history is the receipt a lead needs when someone asks "why them?".
  • Retrieval walks the graph, not embeddings. SelfGraph seeds with BM25 and then traverses. That's exactly the behaviour I wanted: the answer comes from the links between people and work, not from how a bio is worded.
  • The HydraDB engine is the store. It runs locally from the official image and speaks Bolt, so SelfGraph's HydraDBStore works against it with no glue code. The same code also runs on SelfGraph's embedded store, which made iterating fast.

Does it get sharper? The numbers

Track 1 asks for an accuracy curve that rises over iterations, against a real baseline. I measured hire success: the share of tasks where the chosen person actually delivered.

To keep it honest, the outcomes come from hidden true delivery rates that the method never sees. The baseline is profile search over self-written bios. I ran 5 simulated communities of 40 people, each with 10 rounds of 12 tasks. 35% of bios are inflated, and many good contributors have thin bios. Every round's outcomes go back into HydraDB.

Hire success
Vouch Graph, round 1 (cold start: only claims)50%
Vouch Graph, rounds 8–1090%
Profile search, all rounds65%
Oracle that knows the truth95%

Round 1 is worse than profile search, and I left it that way on purpose: with no history, claims are all the graph has. By round 3 it has overtaken the baseline, and by the end it's within five points of the oracle. The curve is on the live page, and eval.py reproduces it.

Technical challenges, and what I did

1. The hosted API needed a paid plan. The HydraDB dashboard only offered paid tiers. The organisers confirmed the hackathon is about the open-source engine, so I switched to running ghcr.io/hydra-db/hydradb locally in Docker: a single local node, a plaintext dev token, and anonymous usage reporting turned off. Then I pointed SelfGraph's HydraDBStore at bolt://127.0.0.1:7687.
2. Facts vanished when I time-travelled. My first as_of run showed lower scores than the live run. The work-to-skill (NEEDED) edges had no event_time, so their effective time was the moment I inserted them, after the date I was asking about. SelfGraph was right and my data was wrong. Passing each work record's real date as event_time on the insert fixed it, and now as_of 31 Oct and the live run agree on everything that existed by then.
3. Not every Cypher query is supported yet. I tried to check the stored graph straight from the Neo4j driver. HydraDB rejected a count() query with "OpenCypher query is not supported yet". A plain MATCH … RETURN … LIMIT worked, which was enough to confirm every entity was in HydraDB under its own namespace.
4. Keeping the baseline fair. My first baseline scored higher than it should have, because I'd normalised by bio length, which quietly punished loud bios. A real vector store rewards keyword-rich bios. Once I made the baseline score plain matches, and gave many honest contributors thin bios like they have in real life, the comparison measured what I care about: whether delivered work beats self-description.
5. Cold start. With no history, the graph has to lean on claims. I score each person with a smoothed delivery rate: a claim is a weak prior that evidence overrides. Ties go to people with less history, so the graph keeps learning about new contributors instead of reusing the same three people.

Who it's for

DAO ops leads, grant programs and ambassador programs hire contributors every month. They already hold the data: payout lists, GitHub co-commits, event rosters and Discord roles. Vouch Graph turns that into contributor search with receipts, sold per community per month. The trust path is what makes a hiring decision defensible.
